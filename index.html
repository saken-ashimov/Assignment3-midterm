A reboot that stayed with Caesar

In 2011, 20th Century Fox restarted the Planet of the Apes series. Instead of copying the 1968 film, the new trilogy showed how the apes rose step by step and placed one character at the centre: Caesar, played through motion capture by Andy Serkis.

The digital effects were crafted by Weta Digital. The apes are not actors in rubber masks: every look, every sign-language gesture, and every pause comes from a real performance.

Each chapter raises the conflict: first a lab escape, then a fragile forest truce, and finally an all-out struggle for survival.

The three films

Rise of the Planet of the Apes

2011 · Director: Rupert Wyatt

A scientist tests an Alzheimer's cure on chimpanzees. His adopted ape Caesar grows smarter than anyone expected and leads a revolt across the Golden Gate Bridge.

Runtime: 105 min · Worldwide Box Office: $481.8M
Key milestone: First film to record outdoor motion-capture scenes in sunlight on the Golden Gate Bridge set.

Dawn of the Planet of the Apes

2014 · Director: Matt Reeves

Ten years later, the Simian Flu has wiped out most people. Apes and surviving humans try to share the forest, until Koba's betrayal destroys the truce.

Runtime: 130 min · Worldwide Box Office: $710.6M
Key milestone: Filmed in real rainy forests around Vancouver and New Orleans with 85% digital ape cast scenes.

War for the Planet of the Apes

2017 · Director: Matt Reeves

Caesar hunts the ruthless Colonel who attacked his family, leading to a snowy final journey where Caesar must choose rescue over revenge.

Runtime: 140 min · Worldwide Box Office: $490.7M
Key milestone: Weta Digital simulated millions of snow-covered hairs interacting with wind and torchlight.
